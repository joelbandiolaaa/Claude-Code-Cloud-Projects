# Case study: Kenneth Villar, BrewedOps

**Status:** Draft. Built from screenshots of brewedops.com (captured 2026-10-28).
**Why this exists:** Same niche as Joel (freelance and ops support). Used as a reference for how a peer packages a process into a site.
**Source:** Public website only. Screenshots are in [`screenshots/`](screenshots/). Anything marked *unverified* was read from a thumbnail or is too small to read and needs a closer look.

---

## 1. Overview

Kenneth Villar runs **BrewedOps**, a brand built around freelance operations. The site leads with a product, **kape.tools** ("Run your whole freelance day from one tab"), and then explains the process behind it through **The Brewed Method**: Catch, Brew, Pour.

The main idea of the whole site: sales happen on their own if the steps run in order. The site sells the system, not just the tool.

## 2. Positioning

| Question | What the site says |
|---|---|
| Who is it for? | Freelancers, mainly people who find work through job boards and referrals |
| What do they sell? | A product (kape.tools) plus the method behind it. The method is the story |
| What is the promise? | Run the whole freelance day from one tab; the process runs without you chasing it |
| What is the tone? | Plain and direct. Headlines are short and confident ("Straight answers.", "Pour in, freelance out.") |

## 3. The Brewed Method

The method is the centerpiece of the page. It is a three-stage sequence shown as a scroll-driven section with a progress track (01 / 03, 02 / 03, 03 / 03).

| Stage | Headline | Line under the headline | Parts shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Catch** | "Catch." | Every lead gets saved. No one slips through. | Forms, DMs, Ads, Referrals |
| **2. Brew** | "Brew." | The system follows up for you, for as long as it takes. | Tags, Emails, Texts |
| **3. Pour** | "Pour." | Ready-to-buy people land in your calendar. You just show up. | Calendar, Inbox, CRM |

Intro line: *"Catch. Brew. Pour. Always in that order. Do the three steps in order and sales happen on their own."*

**Why it works (observed):**
- Each stage has one verb, one promise, and three concrete channels. No stage has more than four items.
- The stages map to a sequence a buyer goes through: capture the lead, keep following up, then book the call.
- Every stage answers "what happens to the people who did not buy yet?" Brew covers that. Most small-business sites skip it.

## 5. Visual and tone

- **Background:** alternates between warm off-white (`#F1EEE6`-ish), warm grey-beige, and near-black. Each section change is a full-bleed color shift.
- **Texture:** scattered dot or grain particles around the method headlines. Decorative, not functional.
- **Type:** a heavy geometric sans for the stage headlines (Poppins-like). Small uppercase labels with wide letter spacing for eyebrows (`[ THE BREWED METHOD ]`, `CATCH · BREW · POUR`).
- **Accent:** a single blue dot used for the active stage on the progress track and for the period in "Pour." It is the only bright color on the page.
- **Icons:** thin outline icons in rounded squares, labeled underneath.

## 6. What to borrow for Joel

These are ideas about structure. Write Joel's own copy.

1. **A three-stage sequence with one verb per stage.** Joel's niche (IT support, websites, photography add-on) can use the same shape. Candidate stages (draft, not final): *Catch* (inquiry in), *Keep* (follow-up and setup), *Support* (ongoing IT help).
2. **"Do the steps in order and results follow."** This line explains why a process matters better than a list of services.
3. **Every stage lists its channels.** This makes each stage concrete, which Joel said he lacks ("walang system na concrete").
4. **A follow-up stage.** Joel's Aprint Lhyn case study covers intake (quote form to CRM) and an automatic reply, but no ongoing follow-up. The Brew stage is the gap.
5. **Short, declarative section headlines.** BRAND.md already asks for one display headline per page, which fits this style.

## 7. What not to copy

- **Exact copy and headlines.** Do not reuse "Catch. Brew. Pour." or "Build it once. Let it run forever." Those are Kenneth's brand. A similar structure with different words is fine.
- **The particle and grain texture.** BRAND.md says one pattern per design, and Joel's pattern is the 30° line and step zigzag from the logo.
- **The blue accent.** Joel's palette is Mustard, Espresso, and Dirty White (BRAND.md). Do not add blue.
- **Three-tone page backgrounds.** BRAND.md says Dirty White for page background. Switching to near-black sections would need a BRAND.md change first.
